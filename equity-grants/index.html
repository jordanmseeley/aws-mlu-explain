Value

“Total amount at stake”

Expressed in currency, converted to shares at grant. Value shifts over time with the stock price—employees are betting on upside.

Instrument

RSUs vs. stock options

RSUs deliver shares at vest with no exercise. Options deliver the right to buy at a strike price—upside heavy but requires action.

Duration

The runway

Most tech plans use 4 years; shorter for make-wholes, longer for founders. Duration encodes how long the incentive should stay live.

Interval

How often equity lands

Monthly feels smooth and employee-friendly. Quarterly and annual intervals feel more like mini-cliffs—stickier for retention.

Cliff

The probationary period

Nothing vests during the cliff; then a lump hits. Commonly 12 months for initial grants; refreshers often skip cliffs entirely.

Curve

The weighting by year

Straight-line is predictable; front-loaded is candidate friendly; back-loaded maximizes stickiness (think 5-15-40-40).

Building blocks

Each gear is standard—the arrangement is the art

Combine these pieces to match your philosophy. The mechanics rarely change—but the weighting and narrative do.

Value

  • Total notional at grant date
  • Share count shifts with price
  • Employees bet on future upside

Instrument

  • RSUs: no exercise, taxed at vest
  • Options: strike price, taxed at exercise
  • PSUs: tie to milestones or metrics

Cliff & interval

  • Cliff defers first vest (often 12 months)
  • Intervals set the cadence (monthly feels smooth)
  • Quarterly/annual can feel like mini-cliffs

Curve & duration

  • Standard: flat 25/25/25/25 over 4 years
  • Front-loaded: more early, wins candidates
  • Back-loaded: stickiness for later years

Curves

Weighting changes the story, not the math

A. Straight-line

Flat and predictable

Classic 25/25/25/25 or 1/48th monthly. Easy to model, easy to explain, steady delivery.

B. Front-loaded

Early win, taper later

40-30-20-10 or 33-33-22-12. Psychologically generous up front, great for candidate attraction.

C. Back-loaded

Stickiness by design

5-15-40-40 or 5-10-35-50. Maximum retention in later years, often paired with higher total grant values.

D. Make-whole variants

Compressed, front-heavy

Designed to replace forfeited equity. Shorter duration, partial immediate vest, and accelerated early curve.

Grant contexts

Same mechanics, different purposes

Initial grants

  • Almost always have a cliff
  • Commonly 4-year duration
  • Front/back-load to shape total comp

Refreshers

  • Often no cliff
  • Even vesting over 2–4 years
  • Cadenced annually to avoid equity cliffs

Make-wholes

  • Replace forfeited equity
  • Shorter duration; partial immediate vest
  • Compressed, front-loaded schedule

Vesting basis

What actually triggers vesting?

Time-based

The most common for tech RSUs/options. Stay employed, vest over time.

Performance-based

Executive PSUs tied to revenue, margin, shipped products, or stock price hurdles.

Milestone-based

Founders/advisors vest on specific milestones—launch, fundraise, or certifications.

Double-trigger (M&A)

Requires acquisition event plus termination/“good reason”. Safety net for employees.

Translate the gears into your plan

Start with the standard gears, then tune the curve and interval to match your talent narrative. Be explicit about cliffs, refreshes, and how value is modeled.
